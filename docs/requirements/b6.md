# B6 unattended close and orchestration register rows (CoCoLo 0.4.0)

Source: `~/Code/temp/orchestrator-child-liveness/ANALYSIS-0.4.0-rsb-b4-b7.md` section 6 (RS-B-6, RS-5 orchestration
part, SH-6 cleanup). Decisions: 131 (this slice carries the next project-history schema step, 20 -> 21, with RS-D S3's
partial index for the retirement pass; S3's parked red tests come in from `feat/rsd-s3-budgets` 9a3c6860 and turn green
here), 129.1 (memory holds are their own slice after B6: not checked here, status keeps `not_tracked`), 185/187 (an
indexed orchestration state column in the same step, so counting open or closed orchestrations needs no checkpoint

**RS-B-B6-1 — Every orchestration has a register row.**
> Every orchestration has a register row: taken when created, released `closed` by the primary's close or
> `end_earlier`, `interrupted_unattended` by the unattended close, each in the close's own transaction. Existing open
> orchestrations are adopted in a 20 -> 21 step only; the validator then refuses an open orchestration without a held row.

Acceptance: `orchestration.open` writes, in its own transaction, a held `orchestration` row (identity = handle,
`taken_at_ms` = open time, `due_at_ms` = open time + ORCHESTRATION_IDLE_MS). Every later non-close mutation
(checkpoint, pause, resume) moves `due_at_ms` to its own time + ORCHESTRATION_IDLE_MS. The primary's close releases it
`closed` (evidence `{"by":"primary"}`), `end_earlier` releases it `closed` (evidence `ended_by_update_unrecorded`, as
RS-D N1), the unattended close releases it `interrupted_unattended` (evidence `{"by":"unattended"}`). A failure writing
the release rolls the close back (the orchestration stays open, its row held). The register validator (SH-7, run at
open, at restore and inside the step) refuses: an open orchestration without a held row; a held row whose orchestration
is closed or missing. An ordinary open never adopts.

**RS-B-B6-2 — Eligibility of the unattended close, inside its transaction.**
> No held lease or reservation of that orchestration, `_unfinished` false, effective now (`max(wall, floor)`) >=
> `due_at_ms` (24 h after last activity). Memory kinds are not checked: a live registered run with no lease is refused
> new work by the existing `active_for` gates after the close.

Acceptance: inside the close's transaction the unattended close refuses (`history_orchestration_pending`, nothing
written) when any reservation of the orchestration has a held or releasing register row, when `_unfinished` is true
(covers held leases: a held lease row is exactly a live lease, SH-7), or when effective now < `due_at_ms`. Effective now
is `max(wall, persisted floor)` read without moving the floor: a wall clock moved back below the floor still closes at
the floor. Taking an execution hold (lease or reservation) of the orchestration is activity: `due_at_ms` moves to at
least its time + ORCHESTRATION_IDLE_MS, in the take's own transaction (releasing one is not activity, B6-4). After the
close, `active_for` refuses new work for that orchestration
(`history_orchestration_state`).

**RS-B-B6-3 — A crash-replay of the unattended close is the identical request.**
Acceptance: the unattended close's request id is derived from (orchestration handle, expected sequence) with the
store's key (`end-idle-<32 hex>`), never random. Applying the same derived request again answers `known: true` with the
same receipt and writes nothing; two passes or two stores built from the same state derive the same id.

**RS-B-B6-4 — Fairness: next check, back-off, reset.**
Acceptance: one pass selects held orchestration rows by `next_check_ms` (ascending, at most UNATTENDED_CLOSE_BATCH = 4
per pass; the service passes 4), only rows with `next_check_ms <= now` and `due_at_ms <= now`, through a partial index
(`project_holds_orchestration_check`). A row that is due but refused (B6-2) backs off: its first refusal sets
`next_check_ms = now + 10 s`, each later one doubles the wait, capped at 5 min. Releasing any execution hold (lease or
reservation) of that orchestration resets its `next_check_ms` to 0 (and the back-off) in the release's own transaction.
A blocked row never stops a later due row from closing in the same pass.

**RS-B-B6-5 — Cleanup rows of closed orchestrations (K1, K2).**
Acceptance K1: a never-admitted attempt (lease released `withdrawn_unsent`, no reservation) of a closed orchestration,
past its reserved time + RESULT_PURGE_GRACE_MS, has its lease and cleanup rows purged by the expiry pass, through its
register release: the lease row's released tombstone stays, and the orchestration's row stays released.
K2: an admitted attempt (reservation exists, committed and recorded) of a closed orchestration with no account release
acknowledgement and no settlement is never purged, however late: its reservation, lease and cleanup rows stay and its
reservation register row stays held.

**RS-B-B6-6 — Changed runtime (RS-D N1, N2).**
Built by RS-D S2 (`docs/requirements/rsd-runtime-close.md`); re-run green here on the new step: eligible -> closed and
custody `orchestration_closed`, unrecorded ones counted; unfinished stays open with custody kept.

**RS-B-B6-7 — Schema step 20 -> 21 (decision 131).**
Acceptance: the store format is 21. The step is additive: it adds `project_orchestrations.state`
(`TEXT NOT NULL DEFAULT 'open'`, `open` or `closed`), the index `project_orchestrations_state(project_id,state)`, the
partial index `result_reservations_live(project_id) WHERE state<>'expired'` (RS-D S3) and the partial index
`project_holds_orchestration_check(project_id,next_check_ms) WHERE kind='orchestration' AND state='held'` (B6-4). It
runs only on the authorized upgrade path (an ordinary open of a 20 store is refused, `history_incompatible`, the file
unchanged), as ONE transaction with the existing step machinery: copy kept first (`history.sqlite3.before-21.*`, same
backup format), every DDL, the state backfill, the adoption (each open orchestration without a row gets a held row,
evidence `{"adopted":21}`, `due_at_ms` = last activity + 24 h; closed ones and existing tombstones are left as they are)
and both validators before commit. Any failure (process death included) leaves the store at 20, its rows unchanged
and the copy removed; a later attempt upgrades. A repeated open of the 21 store changes nothing. A 20 store with an
unknown object (trigger, table, index) is refused before any write. Records-upgrade: a real store written by the
step-20 runtime (0.8.57, `tests/fixtures/history_step20/store.sql`, generated by `make_store.py` with that runtime)
upgrades with every pre-existing row byte-identical (orchestrations gain only `state`), its orchestrations' states
match their checkpoints, the active and paused ones are adopted held, and the closed ones still close-, purge- and
report- as before.

**RS-B-B6-8 — Indexed orchestration state (decisions 185, 187).**
Acceptance: every open/close writes `state` in the same statement as the checkpoint pointer. Counting open
orchestrations (`_open_count`, the open limit) reads only `project_orchestrations_state`, never a checkpoint blob
(work equal at 0 and 1,000 closed orchestrations, measured as SQLite VM steps). Integration note: batch 3's
`OrchestrationCountsCostTests` (not on this base) is to be un-skipped against this column when batches merge.

## Review round 1 (Codex security review of 700d41f8, NOT-READY): added acceptance

- RS-B-B6-7 (HIGH adoption idle window): the step's last activity of an open orchestration is the latest of its
  checkpoint and every attributable attempt activity: its reservations' `created_at_ms` and register `taken_at_ms`
  (a bound reservation's take is its bind), its leases' `created_at_ms` and register `taken_at_ms` (a lease withdrawn
  unsent with no reservation counts). An existing held orchestration row with no `due_at_ms` gets that due time (its
  take and evidence unchanged). Proven on `tests/fixtures/history_step20/store_leases.sql` (same step-20 runtime,
  `make_store.py --leases`).
- RS-B-B6-2 (HIGH retained history): a close's unfinished checks read only the target orchestration's own leases and
  reservations, through two step-21 indexes (`result_reservations_orchestration(project_id,orchestration_handle)`,
  `result_leases_orchestration(project_id, json_extract(CAST(binding AS TEXT),'$.orchestration_handle'))`), each still
  verified by its seal (consumption, grant, receipt evidence as before); MAX_LEASE_ROWS bounds the target's own rows
  only. A complete primary close and a complete unattended close do equal SQLite work at 0 and 100,000 retained
  finished rows of other orchestrations.
- RS-B-B6-2 (MED clock): the unattended close reads its own effective now (`max(wall, floor)`, the floor never moved)
  and reads it again inside each close's transaction; the service pass passes no time. A wall clock below the floor
  still closes at the floor, through the service's expire pass.
- RS-B-B6-7 (MED crash copy): each upgrade attempt leaves a marker naming its copy's device and inode before the copy
  is published; a later attempt of the unchanged store removes that attempt's own copy (and temporary file) and no
  other; after a process death and a retry exactly one copy is kept.
- RS-B-B6-7 (MED layout): the schema comparison folds case and whitespace outside quoted literals only; a literal that
  differs (even only in case) is refused, at the step and at an ordinary open.
- RS-B-B6-8 (MED state): at every open (so every restore) and inside the step, each orchestration's `state` agrees
  with its latest sealed checkpoint (one that does not verify is left to the readers); routine counts stay indexed.
- RS-B-B6-4 (LOW): an explicit `limit` above UNATTENDED_CLOSE_BATCH is clamped to it.

## Final fix (decision 210: one targeted fix of fixed scope, accepted on behavioural red -> green, no further round)

- RS-B-B6-2 (item 1, HIGH): a close selects held leases by verified identities, never by the orchestration named in
  an unverified binding: every live lease (`lease_expiry`, state covered by its seal) and every held lease register
  row, which must be the same set; each is read and verified by its seal before its orchestration is compared. One
  whose seal or handle does not verify refuses the close (`history_incompatible`); the lease stays held. Bounded by
  the live leases (MAX_LEASE_ROWS), not by retained history.
- RS-B-B6-2 (item 2): a consumption of the target (its grant names the orchestration; step 21 index
  `project_execution_consumptions_orchestration`) with neither its reservation nor a lease of its claim refuses the
  close, as does a consumption without a reservation of a claim one of the target's leases names. Index seek only;
  the equal-work test at 0 and 100,000 retained rows holds (its consumptions name their owners).
- RS-B-B6-7 (item 3): the attempt marker (random name) carries in its content the copy's name, its temporary file's
  name, the copy's size and sha256, written and fsynced (with the folder) before the copy is published. Recovery
  removes those files only when each matches that content exactly and has no link beyond those two names; a stale
  marker after inode reuse, or a copy with another hard link, is left alone.
- RS-B-B6-8 (item 4): at every open (so every restore) and inside the step, an orchestration whose checkpoint pointer
  does not verify refuses the store (`history_incompatible`), whatever its state column says.
- Item 5: the 4,096-row bound is proven by its own test with 4,097 VALID-seal leases and reservations of another
  orchestration (red on 700d41f8 by the bound alone).

- Decision 212 (RS-B-B6-2): every held or releasing reservation is read by its register row and verified by its seal
  (which covers its orchestration column) before the target's reservations are selected by that column; a missing or
  unverifiable one refuses the close (primary and unattended). Bounded by held reservation rows (MAX_LEASE_ROWS).
- Decision 212 (RS-B-B6-7): before an attempt, this store's own stale temporary copies (this format's exact
  temporary name, regular, one link, owned by this user) are removed; nothing else is touched.
